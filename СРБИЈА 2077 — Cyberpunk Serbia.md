## Tesla is on the banknote

Nikola Tesla was born in 1856 in Smiljan, then part of the Austrian Empire and now in Croatia, to a Serb family. Serbia claims him as its own: his ashes rest in a gilded sphere at the Nikola Tesla Museum in Belgrade, the main airport carries his name, and his face is on the 100-dinar note.

Fitting for a country where the lights stay on all night.

## Street data

The basics, if you're jacking in for the first time.

Script

Serbian is written in both Cyrillic and Latin. Cyrillic is the official script, and you'll see both on every street. Use the toggle at the top to flip the page.

Credits

The dinar (RSD). Cash is still common at markets and small shops.

Fuel

Ćevapi (grilled minced-meat sausages) with kajmak, burek from a bakery at 3 a.m., and a shot of rakija. Plum rakija is called šljivovica.

Night shift

Splavovi are floating clubs moored along the Sava and Danube in Belgrade. They run from dusk to sunrise in summer.

Architecture

Yugoslav brutalism: the Genex Tower, Sava Centar, and the abstract Spomenik monuments scattered across the region.
